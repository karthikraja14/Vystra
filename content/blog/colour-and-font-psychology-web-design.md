Colour and type aren't decoration — they're the first thing a visitor feels, before they read a word. Used well, they build trust and guide action. Here's the practical version.

## What colours signal

- **Blue** — trust, calm, reliability (banks, tech, healthcare).
- **Green** — growth, health, money, nature.
- **Black/gold** — premium, luxury, authority.
- **Red/orange** — energy, urgency (great for calls to action).
- **Purple** — creativity, premium.

There are no absolute rules — context and culture matter — but these associations are strong starting points.

## Using colour with intent

1. **One dominant brand colour**, used consistently.
2. **A contrasting accent** reserved for buttons and calls to action.
3. **Plenty of neutral space** so the important things stand out.

If everything is bright, nothing draws the eye. Restraint is what makes a CTA pop.

## Font psychology

- **Serif** (traditional, trustworthy, editorial).
- **Sans-serif** (modern, clean, approachable).
- **Display fonts** — sparingly, for headlines only.

Two fonts is usually enough: one for headings, one for body. More looks chaotic.

## Readability first
Fancy fonts that are hard to read cost you readers. Size, spacing and contrast matter more than personality.

## The takeaway
Colour and type should express your brand *and* guide the visitor's eye to what matters. At Vystra we design these choices around your positioning and your conversion goals — not just what looks nice.
