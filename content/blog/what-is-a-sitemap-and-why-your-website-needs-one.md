A sitemap is a simple file that lists all the pages on your website. Think of it as a table of contents you hand to Google so it can find and index everything.

## Why it matters
- **Discovery.** Google finds your pages faster — especially new ones and pages not linked from your menu.
- **Understanding.** It tells search engines your site's structure and which pages exist.
- **Coverage.** Without one, some pages may never get indexed — meaning they can't appear in search at all.

## The two kinds
- **XML sitemap** — for search engines. Lives at yoursite.com/sitemap.xml.
- **HTML sitemap** — a page for human visitors (optional, less common now).

The XML one is what matters for SEO.

## How to make sure it's working
1. Visit yoursite.com/sitemap.xml — you should see a list of your pages.
2. Submit it in **Google Search Console** under the Sitemaps report.
3. Reference it in your robots.txt file.

## A common mistake
People paste their sitemap URL into Google's *URL Inspection* tool and see errors. That's the wrong tool — sitemaps go in the **Sitemaps** report, not URL Inspection.
