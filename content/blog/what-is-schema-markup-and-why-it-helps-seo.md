Schema markup is extra code that tells search engines *what your content means* — not just what it says. It's invisible to visitors, but it helps Google display your site better in search results.

## What it does
Plain text tells Google "here are some words." Schema says "this is a **business**, with this **address**, these **reviews**, this **price**, and these **FAQs**." That understanding can unlock richer search listings.

## The payoff: rich results
With schema, your listing can show:
- ⭐ **Star ratings** from reviews.
- **FAQ dropdowns** right in the results.
- **Business info** — hours, location, phone.
- **Prices** and availability.

These make your listing bigger and more clickable than plain blue links.

## Common types for small businesses
- **LocalBusiness** — name, address, hours.
- **Product** — price and availability.
- **Review / AggregateRating** — star ratings.
- **FAQPage** — questions and answers.
- **Article** — for blog posts.

## Does it directly boost rankings?
Not directly — but the richer, more eye-catching listings earn more clicks, and it helps AI search tools understand and cite your business. Both matter more every year.
