Big, unoptimised images are the most common reason websites load slowly. A single photo straight from a phone camera can be several megabytes — enough to make a page crawl on mobile data.

## Why it matters
- **Speed** — images are usually the heaviest part of a page.
- **SEO** — Google ranks fast sites higher.
- **Bounce** — slow pages lose visitors before they even load.

## The three things that matter

**1. Right size (dimensions).**
Don't upload a 4000px photo to display at 800px. Resize it to the size it's actually shown.

**2. Right format.**
- **WebP** — best for most photos (small and sharp).
- **JPEG** — fine for photos.
- **PNG** — for logos and graphics with transparency.
- **SVG** — for icons and logos (scales perfectly, tiny).

**3. Compression.**
Run images through a compressor to cut file size — often 70% smaller with no visible quality loss.

## A quick win
"Lazy loading" makes images load only as the visitor scrolls to them, so the top of the page appears instantly.
