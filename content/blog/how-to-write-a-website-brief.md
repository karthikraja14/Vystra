The single biggest cause of expensive, delayed website projects is a vague brief. Twenty minutes writing a good one saves weeks of back-and-forth. Here's the structure.

## 1. Your business in one line
What you do, for whom. This anchors every decision.

## 2. The goal of the site
Be specific: get enquiries, sell products, book appointments, look credible. One primary goal beats five vague ones.

## 3. Your audience
Who visits, and what they need from you. A B2B buyer and a walk-in customer need different sites.

## 4. Pages you need
List them: Home, About, Services, Contact, Blog… If you're unsure, list the questions customers ask — each cluster often becomes a page.

## 5. Examples you like
Two or three sites you admire, and *why* — layout, tone, simplicity. This communicates taste faster than paragraphs.

## 6. Content status
Do you have text, logo, photos? Content delays cause most project slippage.

## 7. Practical details
- **Budget range** — so proposals fit reality.
- **Deadline** — and what's driving it.
- **Who decides** — one decision-maker keeps things moving.
