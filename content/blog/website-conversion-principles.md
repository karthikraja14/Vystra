A website's job is not to win design awards. Its job is to turn a stranger into a customer. Here are the seven things that move that needle, in order of impact.

## 1. A headline that states the outcome

Visitors decide in about three seconds whether to stay. Your headline should say what you do and what the customer gets — not a clever pun. "Websites that print revenue" beats "Welcome to our site."

## 2. One clear call to action

Every extra choice lowers conversion. Pick one primary action ("Start a project", "Book a call", "Buy now") and repeat it. Secondary links are fine; competing buttons are not.

## 3. Speed under two seconds

Every additional second of load time drops conversions measurably. Compress images, ship minimal JavaScript, and aim for a 90+ Lighthouse score. Speed is also a direct Google ranking factor.

## 4. Proof, early and specific

"Trusted by thousands" is weak. "+180% leads in one month" with a named client is strong. Put real numbers, reviews and logos above the fold.

## 5. Mobile-first, always

More than half of traffic is mobile. If your buttons are hard to tap or text is tiny on a phone, you are losing the majority of your visitors before they read a word.

## 6. Remove friction from the ask

Every form field costs you conversions. Ask only for what you need to start a conversation — usually a name, an email and a one-line brief.

## 7. Design that builds trust in a glance

Consistent spacing, a real brand identity and no stock-photo clichés signal that you are legitimate. Trust is a conversion feature.
