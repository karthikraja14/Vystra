A single-page website puts everything — services, about, contact — on one scrolling page instead of separate pages. It's simpler and cheaper, but it isn't right for everyone.

## What it is
Instead of clicking between Home, About, Services and Contact, visitors scroll down one long page. The menu links jump to sections on the same page.

## When a single-page site works well
- **A simple, focused business** — one main service or offer.
- **A new business** getting online quickly and affordably.
- **A landing page** for a specific campaign or product.
- **You want visitors to follow one story** from top to bottom.

## When you need multiple pages
- **Many services** that each deserve their own detailed page.
- **You publish a blog** or lots of content.
- **You want stronger SEO** — more pages means more chances to rank for different searches.
- **An online store** with products.

## The SEO trade-off
This is the main catch: a single page can only realistically rank for a limited set of keywords. Multiple pages let you target many different searches — a big advantage as you grow.

## A common path
Many businesses start with a clean single-page site, then expand to multiple pages as they grow and need more SEO reach. That's a perfectly good strategy.

## The takeaway
A single-page site is a smart, affordable start for simple, focused businesses — but multi-page wins for SEO and larger offerings. At Vystra we recommend the right structure for where your business is now and where it's heading.
