A hacked website isn't just embarrassing — it can leak customer data, spread malware, get you blacklisted by Google, and destroy trust overnight. The good news: most attacks exploit basic gaps you can close.

## Why small businesses get targeted
Hackers don't pick you personally — bots scan the whole internet for easy targets. An unmaintained site with old software is exactly what they look for.

## The security basics

**1. Keep everything updated.**
Outdated software and plugins are the #1 way sites get hacked. Update promptly.

**2. Use HTTPS (SSL).**
Encrypts data between your site and visitors. It's essential and expected — browsers warn users away from sites without it.

**3. Strong passwords + 2FA.**
Weak admin passwords are an open door. Use strong, unique passwords and turn on two-factor authentication.

**4. Regular backups.**
If the worst happens, a recent backup lets you restore quickly. Without one, recovery can be impossible.

**5. Limit access.**
Only give admin access to those who need it. Fewer doors, fewer risks.

## Warning signs you've been compromised
- Strange redirects or pop-ups.
- Unknown pages or content appearing.
- Google flags your site as "not secure" or "deceptive."
- Sudden slowdowns.

## The simplest protection of all
Ongoing maintenance. A well-maintained site with current software, HTTPS and backups sidesteps the vast majority of attacks.
