Most bad website experiences start at the hiring stage — vague scope, no ownership, and a "cheap" quote that balloons later. Here's how to choose well.

## Look at outcomes, not just portfolios

A pretty portfolio is table stakes. Ask what the sites **achieved** — more leads, more sales, faster load. A studio that talks only about visuals may not think about results.

## Questions worth asking

- **Do I own the code, domain and accounts** when we're done?
- **What exactly is included** — content, SEO setup, revisions, training?
- **How do you handle changes** after launch?
- **What's the timeline**, and what happens if it slips?
- **Can I speak to a past client?**

## Red flags

- No written scope or contract.
- Won't hand over ownership of your assets.
- A quote far below everyone else (someone pays for it later — usually you).
- No questions about **your** business or goals.

## Comparing quotes fairly

Don't compare on price alone. Line up scope, ownership, support and timeline side by side. The cheapest quote is often the most expensive once revisions, hosting lock-in and rework are added.

## Agency vs freelancer vs studio

Freelancers are affordable but a single point of failure. Big agencies bring process but high cost and slow pace. A focused studio often sits in the sweet spot — senior work, direct communication, fixed pricing.
