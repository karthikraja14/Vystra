Images do a huge amount of work on a website — they set the mood, build trust and show what you do. The wrong images make even a well-built site feel cheap or generic.

## Real beats stock
The single biggest upgrade: use **real photos** of your actual work, team and premises instead of generic stock images. People can spot stock photos instantly, and they create distance. Real photos build trust.

## What makes a good website image
- **Relevant** — it shows *your* work or something meaningful, not a random smiling model.
- **High quality** — sharp and well-lit, not blurry or dark.
- **Consistent** — a similar style and tone across the site.
- **Optimised** — sized and compressed so it loads fast.

## Where real photos matter most
- **Your work / products** — customers want to see the real thing.
- **Your team** — faces build trust for small businesses.
- **Your premises** — shows you're a real, established business.

## When you do need stock
Sometimes you need a background or concept image. If so:
- Choose natural, non-cheesy photos.
- Keep the style consistent.
- Avoid the obvious overused ones.

## A quick tip
A decent smartphone in good light can take great photos of your work — often better than any stock image, because it's *yours*.
