A fast website feels effortless. A slow one loses customers before they read a word — and Google notices too. Here's what actually makes a site fast, without the jargon.

## Why speed is worth money

- **Conversions.** Every extra second of load time measurably drops sales and enquiries.
- **Rankings.** Google uses speed (Core Web Vitals) as a direct ranking signal.
- **Trust.** Fast feels professional; slow feels broken.

Speed isn't a nice-to-have. It's a revenue and visibility feature.

## What actually makes a site fast

1. **Optimised images.** Photos are usually the heaviest thing on a page. Compressing and correctly sizing them is the single biggest win.
2. **Minimal, efficient code.** Bloated templates and dozens of plugins pile on weight. Lean, purpose-built code loads faster.
3. **Good hosting and a CDN.** Serving your site from servers close to your visitors cuts delay.
4. **Loading only what's needed.** Deferring non-essential scripts so the page appears before everything finishes loading.
5. **Caching.** Reusing already-loaded assets so repeat visits are near-instant.

## How to check your own speed

Run your URL through [Google PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev). Aim for a score of 90+ on mobile. It'll also list exactly what's slowing you down.

## The catch with DIY and template sites

Many builder and template sites are slow by default because they load heavy, generic code you can't fully control. A custom-built, performance-first site avoids that — it ships only what your site actually needs.
