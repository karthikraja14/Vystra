Alt text (alternative text) is a short written description attached to each image on your website. Visitors don't normally see it — but search engines and screen readers rely on it.

## What it does
Computers can't "see" a photo. Alt text tells them what the image shows. It serves two jobs:

- **Accessibility** — screen readers read it aloud to blind and low-vision users.
- **SEO** — it helps Google understand your images and can get them ranked in Google Image search.

It also displays if an image fails to load.

## How to write good alt text
- **Describe the image plainly.** "Two-storey house with a red brick exterior," not "image123."
- **Be specific but concise.** One clear sentence is usually enough.
- **Include a keyword naturally** *if* it genuinely fits — never stuff keywords.
- **Skip "image of…"** — screen readers already announce it's an image.

## When to leave it empty
Purely decorative images (background flourishes) can have empty alt text so screen readers skip them. Meaningful images should always have it.

## A common mistake
Leaving every image with no alt text — or stuffing keywords into it. Both hurt. Natural, accurate descriptions are the goal.

## The takeaway
Alt text is a small habit that improves both your SEO and your site's accessibility — two things Google increasingly rewards. At Vystra we add proper alt text as standard, so your images work for search and for every visitor.
